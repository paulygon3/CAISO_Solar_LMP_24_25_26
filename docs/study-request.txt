2. Canonical: normalized identifiers, timestamps, units, revisions, and components.

Never overwrite original values with repaired values.

Every repaired or inferred value must remain traceable to its source, rule, and processing version.
Build an expected-hour scaffold and classify missingness before estimating anything.

Distinguish:
- Failed or incomplete retrieval.
- Unpublished or unavailable source observation.
- Invalid source value.
- Duplicate or revision conflict.
- Timestamp or calendar-mapping error.
- Metadata join failure.
- Unresolved solar-zone assignment.
- Missing modeled solar output.
- Internal node-price gap.
- Pre-observation or post-observation node availability.
- Widespread simultaneous gap.
For each gap, record:
- Node, zone, model location, or dataset affected.
- UTC start/end.
- Pacific timestamps.
- Consecutive missing-hour count.
- Month and requested analysis periods.
- Share of expected hours affected.
- Relevant solar-weight exposure, where estimable.
- Established cause or UNKNOWN.
- Proposed action.
Examine whether gaps cluster by hour, month, node type, zone, BAA, market regime, or source batch.

Do not assume missing observations are random. Do not infer retirement from disappearing prices.
Apply this order:
First:
- Retry the source/model request.
- Verify the returned schema and units.
- Regenerate the modeled profile.
- Correct timestamp, interval-label, leap-day, or year-boundary mapping problems.
- Recover the original model output where possible.
Recovering an original modeled value is not statistical imputation.
Treat February 29 mapping as a deterministic modeling assumption.

Flag it separately from unexpected missing-output repair.

Do not count planned leap-day synthesis as an unexplained source gap.
A gap is not acceptable to fill merely because it is short.

Assess:
- Consecutive duration.
- Position in the solar day.
- Neighboring valid values.
- Seasonal context.
- Whether the gap spans sunrise, sunset, or a steep production ramp.
- Whether other modeled locations are also missing.
- Potential influence on price-weighted results.
As initial screening limits, evaluate gaps of no more than two consecutive hourly observations and no more than 0.5% of expected hours per location-month.

These are configurable project screening limits—not industry standards or automatic approval criteria.

If reliable repair requires broader assumptions, leave the gap unresolved and report the blocker.
For a multi-location zonal profile, repair individual modeled-location series before applying the fixed location weights.

Do not silently renormalize the composite over whichever locations happen to be available. That would change the represented generation mix over time.

If location-level repair is not possible, flag the affected composite hours and evaluate them explicitly.
Evaluate a small, justified set of methods, such as:
- Linear interpolation for isolated internal gaps.
- Corresponding-hour estimates from nearby calendar days.
- Estimates using other modeled locations with demonstrated relationships.
Do not assume one method is best in every season or time of day.

Set a missing hour to zero only when independently supported as a zero-production hour by solar geometry/model evidence. Missingness itself is not evidence of zero production.

Preserve valid model variability. Do not smooth all profiles merely because a repair method is available.
Before applying a statistical repair:
1. Select fully observed profile segments.
2. Hide observations in blocks resembling actual gaps.
3. Reconstruct them using candidate methods.
4. Compare estimates with the hidden original values.
5. Repeat across seasons, solar-day positions, and all three zones.
Where a method is fitted or tuned, separate fitting/tuning data from evaluation blocks. Do not score performance on the same observations used to select parameters.

If neighboring observations on both sides are used, label the method as retrospective gap repair, not forecasting.

Evaluate at least:
- Mean absolute error.
- Root mean square error.
- Mean signed error.
- Error normalized by AC capacity.
- Reconstructed energy bias.
- Sensitivity during sunrise/sunset and high-output periods.
- Change in solar-weighted LMP and matched hub basis.
Do not use percentage error measures that divide by zero or near-zero nighttime generation.

Set numerical acceptance criteria before selecting and applying the repair. Explain their units and analytical relevance.

If no method meets the criteria, leave the gap unresolved. Do not relax thresholds merely to obtain a complete profile.
For every accepted solar-profile repair, report:
- Repaired-hour count.
- Repaired-hour share.
- Solar-weight share attributable to repaired hours.
- Method.
- Validation performance.
- Affected zones, months, and annual/matched periods.
Calculate sensitivity of final prices to the repair.

Compare:
1. Results using the accepted repaired profile.
2. Results excluding the repaired timestamps from both the price numerator and matched-weight denominator.
Label the second calculation as a changed-support sensitivity, not an independently known true result.

Report changes in:
- Solar-weighted LMP.
- Matched hub basis.
- Negative-price exposure.
- Heatmap eligibility, if affected.
Separate the magnitude of missingness from the magnitude of its effect. A small count of missing hours can still matter if those hours have substantial solar output or unusual prices.

If an unresolved solar gap prevents a reliable full-period denominator, do not report apparently complete solar coverage.
For the primary analysis:
- Recover missing published prices through verified official retrieval where possible.
- Correct processing mistakes.
- Preserve remaining missing prices.
- Use the documented valid-price denominator.
- Keep observed coverage distinct from any optional estimated availability.
Do not linearly interpolate LMPs, forward-fill them, replace them with hub prices, or treat them as zero in primary results.

If an optional price-imputation scenario is later requested, it requires its own validation, labels, coverage accounting, and sensitivity analysis. Permission to repair modeled production does not authorize price imputation.
Verify applicable market definitions and component structures by date, BAA, and product.

Do not assume that every node and year shares the same energy component or exactly the same decomposition.

Use the source’s published definitions to distinguish:
- Total LMP.
- Energy.
- Congestion.
- Marginal losses.
- Applicable GHG or other components.
- Unexplained reconciliation residual.
For node-to-hub analysis:

total_basis = node_LMP − hub_LMP

On identical matched timestamps and using identical node-zone weights, decompose this into published component differences where valid:

total_basis = energy_difference + congestion_difference + loss_difference + applicable_other_component_differences

Do not call total basis “congestion” unless the other contributions have been shown to cancel or are explicitly excluded.

If components are missing, preserve total basis but flag the decomposition as incomplete.

Do not infer a particular binding constraint, outage, or physical cause from nodal prices alone. State what additional evidence would be needed.
Use descriptive analysis appropriate to the requested historical population.

- Median and selected quantiles.
- Minimum and maximum.
- Negative-price frequency.
- Solar-weighted negative exposure.
- Monthly variation.
- Concentration of results in a small number of hours.
- Coverage and mapping composition.
Preserve extreme published observations in primary metrics. Robust summaries are supplements, not permission to remove spikes or negative prices.
